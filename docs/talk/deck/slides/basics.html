Hearts, the card game

Fewer points are better.

Every score in this talk is points taken per deal, so lower numbers and negative differences are good.

The play

Four players. Each passes 3 cards to another, then they play 13 tricks. Follow the suit led; the highest card of it takes the trick.

The points

Each heart is 1 point and the queen of spades is 13, to whoever takes the trick they are in. That is 26 a deal.

A void

Having no cards in a suit. A void player can’t follow, so can discard anything, such as the queen.

Shooting the moon

The exception: take all 26 points and you score −26 instead. Stopping someone else’s moon is called eclipsing.

For anyone who hasn’t played: Hearts is a trick-taking game where you try not to win. When I show a number in this talk, lower is better. Each heart you take is a point against you, and the queen of spades is thirteen. Before the play, each player passes three cards to another. The one twist is shooting the moon: take all 26 points and, in our version, you score minus 26 instead. That is also why the players here average under 6.5 points a deal, which is what 26 shared among four would give. That’s all you need for the rest of the talk.
