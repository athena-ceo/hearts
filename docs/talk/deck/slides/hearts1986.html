HEARTS in 1986: Hearts Expert, A Real Time Sink

  • Written at MIT for 6.871 Expert Systems, spring 1986, with Ramana Rao
  • Interlisp-D on the Xerox Dandelion
  • A networked game with four kinds of player: Human, Clown (plays at random), Conservative (one fixed cautious strategy) and Expert
  • The Expert: 97 weighted rules in KEE, IntelliCorp’s expert system shell
  • Opponents modeled with EMYCIN certainty factors
  • Three strategies: minimizing points, shooting the moon, and eclipsing, which is stopping someone else’s moon

The rule listing, printed 5 May 1986

The name was a joke that turned out to be accurate. The Expert’s thoughts appeared in a window as it played, and that window is the part I’m still proudest of. The eclipse strategy is what you do when you suspect someone else is shooting the moon: you try to stop them. In the scan you can see one of the rules that does this, te.across.shooting.
