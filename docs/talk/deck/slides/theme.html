Use the AI to manage the rules, rather than calling the AI to make decisions.

A neural network, compared with the rules, showed where they were weak: leads, and play while shooting.

Rules written from that kept their reasons, and recovered about 15% of the network’s gain.

It relied on cheap measurement: self-play, and any change replayed on the same deals.

Shown here: AI diagnosing a rule base. Not yet shown: a model writing the new rules itself, checked by the compiler and the benchmark.

This is what we’re working on at Athena. I’m a rationalist at heart: I think many neural nets are a fuzzy approximation of knowledge that’s obscure or hasn’t been made explicit. Comparing a partial rule base with a net is an appealing way to offset the slow, error-prone work of knowledge elicitation. This project is one small test of that view. It supports the method, and it shows the limit: the rules recovered only part of the gain. And it works where you can measure cheaply, which most businesses can’t do as easily as a card game.
