How it was run

  • The game: the 1986 code running in headless Medley, unchanged except for test advice: fixed shuffles in place of H.Shuffle and no pause at the end of each trick. The report checks that every player was dealt the same cards.
  • One long game: the pass direction follows the deal number, and the Expert opponents keep modelling the other players across deals. The over score is set out of reach, so nobody plays for the end of a game, and this doesn't measure that skill.
  • Scoring: a player's points for the deal, with a shot moon scoring -26 and nothing added to the others, as in the 1986 code.
  • Margin: a player's points minus the average of the other three, compared with the Expert on the same deal. Points alone ignore an opponent's moon, which in a real game hurts you.
  • Fallbacks: when a model gave no usable answer the seat played a safe legal card, and that deal still counts.
  • Cost: tokens at each provider's listed price. Local models cost nothing here, apart from the machine.

What this can't tell you

  • Small differences. Comparing on the same deals, the difference from the Expert varies by 8.4 to 12.4 points from deal to deal, so telling apart players 1 point per deal apart would take about 595 deals.
  • What the models could do with more effort. Claude ran at low effort, and the local models are old.
  • How a model plays against other opponents. The field here is three Experts, and results against people or a mixed table could differ.
  • Repeatability of a single run. The models aren't deterministic, and each ran once.
